# Kevin Day - USS Princeton Radar Account

Senior Chief Kevin Day was a radar operator aboard the USS Princeton, an Aegis-equipped cruiser operating as part of the Nimitz Carrier Strike Group off the coast of southern California in November 2004.

Beginning approximately two weeks before the 14 November encounter, Day and his team observed anomalous radar returns on the AN/SPY-1B radar system. The objects appeared at approximately 24,000 metres altitude and descended to sea level in a matter of seconds, far exceeding the performance of any known aircraft.

Day reported tracking as many as 100 of these objects over the two-week period. The objects appeared to be travelling at speeds that conventional aircraft cannot achieve. The radar returns were consistent and repeatable, not characteristic of clutter or false returns.

On 14 November 2004, Day vectored Commander David Fravor and Lieutenant Commander Alex Dietrich to intercept one of these objects. After Fravor's visual encounter with the object, it disappeared from his view. Day then detected the same object on radar approximately 100 kilometres away at the combat air patrol point, having traversed the distance in seconds.

Day has stated that this radar data was subsequently collected by unidentified personnel who arrived aboard the Princeton shortly after the encounter. He described them as arriving by helicopter with no prior notice. The radar data and associated recordings were removed from the ship.

Day has reported experiencing health effects following the extended radar tracking period, though the connection to the events remains unverified.
