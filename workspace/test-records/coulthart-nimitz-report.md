# Nimitz UAP Encounter - Ross Coulthart Investigation

Investigative journalist Ross Coulthart, reporting for NewsNation, conducted an in-depth investigation into the 2004 USS Nimitz UAP encounter. Coulthart interviewed multiple witnesses including Commander David Fravor and Senior Chief Kevin Day.

According to Fravor, he was flying a routine training mission approximately 60 miles off the coast of San Diego when the USS Princeton directed him to investigate an unknown contact. Fravor told Coulthart that upon arriving at the coordinates, he observed a whitish object roughly 40 feet long hovering above a disturbance in the ocean.

Fravor described the object to Coulthart as having no wings, no rotors, no exhaust, and no visible means of propulsion. He estimated it was hovering at approximately 50 feet above the water. When he attempted to intercept it, the object accelerated away at what Fravor described as "ballistic speed" and disappeared within seconds.

Kevin Day, who Coulthart also interviewed, stated that he had been tracking similar objects on the Princeton's AN/SPY-1 radar for roughly two weeks before the Fravor encounter. Day told Coulthart that the objects would appear at about 80,000 feet and drop to sea level almost instantly. Day estimated he tracked over 100 such objects during that period.

Day also told Coulthart that after the encounter, individuals he could not identify arrived aboard the Princeton by helicopter and confiscated the radar recordings. Day described this as highly unusual and stated he had never experienced anything like it in his career.

Coulthart's investigation found that the encounter was corroborated by multiple sensor systems including the Princeton's AN/SPY-1 radar, the ATFLIR infrared targeting pod on Chad Underwood's aircraft, and visual observations from multiple pilots. The FLIR1 video, recorded by Underwood, was officially released by the Pentagon in 2017.

Former AATIP director Lue Elizondo told Coulthart that the Nimitz encounter was one of the cases that convinced him the UAP issue deserved serious government attention. Elizondo resigned from the Department of Defense in 2017 in protest over what he described as excessive secrecy surrounding the UAP programme.
