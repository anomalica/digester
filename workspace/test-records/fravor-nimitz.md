# USS Nimitz Encounter - David Fravor Account

On 14 November 2004, Commander David Fravor was a pilot with VFA-41 (the Black Aces) stationed aboard the USS Nimitz operating off the coast of southern California. He was flying an F/A-18F Super Hornet during a routine training exercise.

The USS Princeton, an Aegis-equipped cruiser operating as part of the Nimitz Carrier Strike Group, had been tracking anomalous radar returns for approximately two weeks prior to the encounter. Senior Chief Kevin Day, the radar operator aboard the Princeton, reported multiple objects descending from approximately 24,000 metres altitude to sea level in a matter of seconds.

Fravor and his wingman, Lieutenant Commander Alex Dietrich, were vectored by the Princeton to investigate a contact approximately 100 kilometres west of their position. Upon arriving at the location, Fravor observed a disturbance in the ocean surface, describing it as an area of whitewater approximately the size of a Boeing 737, with a cross-shaped pattern beneath the surface.

Above the whitewater, Fravor observed a white, oblong object approximately 12 metres in length with no wings, no exhaust plume, no visible means of propulsion, and no flight control surfaces. He described it as resembling a large tic tac mint.

As Fravor descended to investigate, the object appeared to react to his presence. It ascended and mirrored his movements. When Fravor attempted to close on the object by cutting across the circle, the object accelerated and disappeared in less than two seconds. Fravor described the acceleration as beyond anything he had ever witnessed.

The object was subsequently detected on radar by the Princeton approximately 100 kilometres away at the combat air patrol point, having traversed the distance in seconds. This was confirmed by Senior Chief Kevin Day.

Chad Underwood, another pilot from VFA-41, later recorded infrared video of a similar object using the ATFLIR pod on his F/A-18F. This footage, known as the FLIR1 video, was later released by the Department of Defense in 2017.
