The 2004 USS Nimitz [Unidentified Aerial Phenomena (UAP)](/topics/unidentified-aerial-phenomena-uap) Encounter took place on 2004-11-14, when naval aviators from the [USS Nimitz Carrier Strike Group](/organisations/uss-nimitz-carrier-strike-group) observed and attempted to intercept an unidentified aerial object during routine training operations. The event was documented by radar, camera, and four naval aviators.<sup>1</sup> [Commander David Fravor](/people/david-fravor) led the [F/A-18](/objects/fa-18) squadron aboard the [USS Nimitz](/objects/uss-nimitz) at the time,<sup>2</sup> with [Lieutenant Commander Alex Dietrich](/people/alex-dietrich) flying as his wingman.<sup>3</sup>

The encounter was preceded by a week of anomalous radar contacts. During the days leading up to 2004-11-14, the advanced radar systems aboard the [USS Princeton](/objects/uss-princeton) had repeatedly detected multiple [Anomalous Aerial Vehicles (AAVs)](/topics/anomalous-aerial-vehicle-aav) operating beyond the horizon, observed descending approximately 24,000 metres in less than a second.<sup>5</sup> On the day of the encounter, the [USS Nimitz Carrier Strike Group](/organisations/uss-nimitz-carrier-strike-group) was conducting training exercises approximately 160 kilometres south-west of [California San Diego](/places/california-san-diego-usa).<sup>4</sup> Fravor and Dietrich, each accompanied by a weapons system officer in the rear seat of their respective [F/A-18](/objects/fa-18) aircraft, were diverted from the training exercise to investigate the UAP contacts.<sup>6</sup>

Upon arrival in the designated area, the pilots found a patch of disturbed white water, roughly the size of a Boeing 737, agitating the surface of an otherwise calm sea.<sup>7</sup> Above this disturbance, Fravor observed a small white UAP.<sup>8</sup> Dietrich circled at altitude whilst Fravor descended for a closer look.<sup>9</sup> The object, described as approximately the same size as an [F/A-18](/objects/fa-18) but bearing no wings, no markings, and no exhaust plumes, was initially oriented north–south before it abruptly turned to mirror Fravor's flight path as he manoeuvred towards it.<sup>12,11</sup> Fravor assessed that the object appeared to be aware of the presence of his aircraft.<sup>10</sup> Dietrich initially attempted to categorise it as a helicopter or drone before ruling both out.<sup>15</sup> All four aircrew observed the object for approximately five minutes.<sup>13</sup>

As Fravor attempted to close the distance, the UAP accelerated and vanished from visual range.<sup>14</sup> Seconds later, the [USS Princeton](/objects/uss-princeton) reacquired the contact on radar, now positioned approximately 100 kilometres away.<sup>15</sup> A second aircrew subsequently acquired the object briefly using a forward-looking infrared (FLIR) targeting camera before it again departed; this footage was later released as the [Nimitz UAP FLIR Targeting Camera Footage](/documents/nimitz-unidentified-aerial-phenomena-uap-forward-looking-infrared-flir-targeting-camera-footage).<sup>16</sup>

Following the encounter, the aircrew filed reports, but no official action was taken for approximately five years.<sup>17</sup> The case was eventually investigated by [Luis Elizondo](/people/luis-elizondo) under the Advanced Aerospace Threat Identification Program (AATIP), a US Department of Defence programme.<sup>18</sup> Dietrich, who had not spoken publicly about the encounter before appearing on [60 Minutes](/organisations/60-minutes) in 2021,<sup>20</sup> stated that she chose to do so because the flight was conducted in a government aircraft on duty and the information is unclassified.<sup>19</sup> The encounter is widely cited as a pivotal moment after which the US government's public stance on UAP began to shift.<sup>19</sup>
