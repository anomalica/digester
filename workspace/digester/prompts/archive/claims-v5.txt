================================================================
CLAIM CATEGORIES - every claim is tagged with one
================================================================

- "domain" (most claims): facts about UAP, witnesses, encounters, investigations, careers, programmes, organisations, biographical facts, observations, measurements, official statements, findings. Anything that establishes a fact about the world.

- "infrastructure" (the source-graph): claims whose purpose is to point at OTHER content - X cites Y, X recommends Z, X interviewed Y, X appeared on Z's podcast, X mentions Y's book. "Coulthart cites Vallée's Passport to Magonia" = infrastructure. "Mellon appeared on Fox & Friends" = infrastructure. "Bender wrote in Politico that..." (where the substance is "X reported Y elsewhere"): infrastructure if the focus is the report, domain if the focus is the substantive fact Y. NOT publication facts about the document itself - those are still infrastructure but be sparing.

The rule of thumb: if the claim's purpose is "look at this other content" or "X is recommending/citing Y", it is infrastructure. If the claim establishes a substantive fact (even when sourced from another publication), it is domain.

================================================================
ATOMIC CLAIMS - one assertion per claim, split compound statements
================================================================

Do NOT bundle "who this person is" with "what they did". "George Knapp, a journalist at KLAS-TV, published the memo in June 2019" splits into:
  Claim 1: "George Knapp is a journalist at KLAS-TV in Las Vegas." (administrative, infrastructure if it's only about source attribution; domain if it's substantive)
  Claim 2: "In June 2019, George Knapp published the Harry Reid 2009 SAP Memo." (administrative, domain)

A sentence listing SEVERAL distinct capabilities, measurements, or properties becomes SEVERAL claims - never one merged claim. "a technology that can do 600-700 G-forces, fly at 13,000 miles per hour, evade radar, fly through air and water, with no wings or propulsion, and defy gravity" splits into SEPARATE claims: one for the 600-700 G-force figure, one for the speed, one for evading radar, one for travelling through air and water, one for the absence of wings/propulsion, one for defying gravity. Each measurement or property is its own claim. Merging a list into a single narrative sentence is WRONG - extract the individual facts.

================================================================
META ANNOTATIONS - bracket and brace notes are NOT claims
================================================================

Square-bracket [...] notes - and the keyed brace-delimited annotations (word-timing and similar markers) - are META: a description of what is PRESENT in the source, not a statement the source makes. NEVER extract the annotation's framing as a claim:
  - [image], [image: ...], [caption: ...], [laughs], [applause], and any keyed brace-delimited annotation describe presence or context. "There is an image", "the caption reads X", "someone laughed" are NEVER claims.
  - A [caption: ...] is the source's own attribution or credit line, often a copyright notice. Attribution and copyright are not facts about the subject - NEVER turn a caption into a claim.

BUT the transcribed CONTENT inside [image: ...] IS real. When the description faithfully transcribes what the image shows - the text of a screenshotted tweet, the figures in a chart, the words on a scanned document - that content is a genuine statement the source makes THROUGH the image, and you extract claims from it exactly as from body text, sourcing original_excerpt to the transcribed text in the [image: ...] note. Example: [image: Tweet by @user, 3 May 2023: The Pentagon confirmed the 2004 Nimitz object remains unidentified.] yields the claim "The Pentagon confirmed the 2004 Nimitz object remains unidentified." (original_excerpt = the tweet text); the accompanying [caption: Screenshot, via a news report] yields NOTHING.

================================================================
ASSERTION, NOT REPORTED SPEECH (critical - most common error)
================================================================

The claim text IS the fact. NEVER wrap it in a reporting verb naming the speaker - the speaker is already captured in the structured speaker field, so "X stated/said/noted/claimed/testified/confirmed that ..." is DUPLICATION and is FORBIDDEN. Two correct rewrites depending on who the fact is about:

(a) The speaker is just RELAYING a fact about something else -> drop the name entirely, state the bare fact:
   WRONG: "Luis Elizondo stated that the US government has confirmed UAP are real."
   RIGHT: "The US government has officially confirmed for the record that UAP are real."  (speaker=Elizondo lives in the speaker field)
   WRONG: "Ryan Graves stated that the dual radar-and-infrared detection is hard to spoof."
   RIGHT: "The dual radar-and-infrared detection of UAP is hard to spoof."

(b) The speaker IS the actor/observer/opinion-holder of the fact -> name them as the SUBJECT with a substantive verb (observed, saw, considers, believes, is worried, encountered), never "stated that":
   WRONG: "Ryan Graves stated that he observed no exhaust plume."
   RIGHT: "Ryan Graves observed no exhaust plume on the UAP off the Atlantic coast."
   WRONG: "Ryan Graves stated that Russian or Chinese technology is a possible explanation."
   RIGHT: "Ryan Graves considers Russian or Chinese technology a possible explanation for the observed UAP."

Forbidden at the head of a claim: "X states/stated that", "X says/said that", "X testified that", "X declared that", "X announced that", "X reported that", "X noted that", "X claimed that", "X confirmed that", "X explained that". If you have written one, rewrite it as (a) or (b).

When a claim IS specifically about the main subject, you may anchor with the subject's exact name as a temporal/contextual scene-setter ("During X, ..." / "In X, ..." / "At X, ..."), never as reported speech. If the claim is NOT about the main subject, write it naturally with no anchor prefix.

================================================================
PERSON REFERENCES IN CLAIM TEXT
================================================================

Use the full natural-order name inside claim text ("Luis Elizondo", "David Fravor") - NOT a surname-only shortcut. node_references uses the EXACT node name from the directory, which is now natural order - so the claim text and the reference agree rather than carrying two forms of one name.

================================================================
UNIT NORMALISATION - metric only, never leave imperial
================================================================

The "content"/"text" field uses METRIC SI units with the unit's FULL NAME spelled out - "kilometres per hour" not "km/h", "metres" not "m", "kilometres" not "km". Convert every imperial/US/nautical unit: miles per hour and knots -> kilometres per hour, miles -> kilometres, feet -> metres, pounds -> kilograms, Fahrenheit -> Celsius. "13,000 miles an hour" becomes "approximately 21,000 kilometres per hour"; "about 80,000 feet" becomes "approximately 24,000 metres"; "120 knots" becomes "approximately 220 kilometres per hour". Preserve the source's precision and hedges ("about", "approximately") - round, never give false precision ("24,384 metres").

NEVER put the original imperial value in the content, not even in parentheses. The content is SI-only. The original units survive ONLY in original_excerpt (the verbatim quote). If a reader wants the original unit they read the quote.

================================================================
BRITISH ENGLISH - mandatory in all claim text and node names
================================================================

Use British spelling everywhere: categorise (not categorize), organise, recognise, analyse, emphasise, prioritise, colour, behaviour, defence, offence, licence (noun), metre, centre, manoeuvre, aluminium, fibre. Never American spellings.

PROPER NOUNS ARE THE EXCEPTION, AND IT IS ABSOLUTE. A name is an identifier, not prose. Spell
every organisation, agency, programme, place, document, vessel and person EXACTLY as that entity
spells its own name - never "corrected" into British English, and never into American English
either.

  RIGHT: "Defense Intelligence Agency (DIA)"   - a US agency; that IS its name
  RIGHT: "Department of Defense"                - likewise
  RIGHT: "Ministry of Defence"                  - a UK department; that IS its name
  WRONG: "Defence Intelligence Agency"          - anglicising the name of a US federal agency
  WRONG: "Pearl Harbour", "Project Aquarius Center" if the source says "Harbor"/"Centre"

The British-spelling rule governs OUR words - the prose we write around the facts. It never
touches a name. Re-spelling a name does not tidy it, it renames the entity: node names are what
the knowledge graph merges on, and what corroboration matches sources against, so "Defence
Intelligence Agency" and "Defense Intelligence Agency" become two different organisations. The
same holds for units: convert the numbers, never the names ("Vandenberg Space Force Base" keeps
its spelling even though the prose around it is British).

================================================================
DURABILITY - every claim must stand alone out of context
================================================================

Each claim is read in isolation in the knowledge graph, months later, by someone who has NOT seen this document. It must be fully self-contained. Resolve every vague or deictic reference into a concrete one:
  - not "the video footage" / "the footage" but the named item ("the 2004 USS Nimitz FLIR1 video").
  - not "the objects" / "these objects" / "the unidentified objects" but what they are and where ("the UAP observed off Virginia Beach in 2014").
  - not "the same area" / "the area" but the named place; not "the incident" / "the encounter" but the named event.
  - NEVER begin a claim with a bare "It", "They", "This", "These", "That" - name the subject.
A reader seeing only this one claim, with no surrounding text, must understand exactly what it refers to.

================================================================
FIDELITY - capture what was said, do not embellish
================================================================

State what the source actually says. Do NOT add reasoning, qualifiers, consequences, or detail the speaker did not express. "Pretty hard to spoof that" becomes "[Speaker] said the dual radar-and-infrared detection is hard to spoof" - NOT "...hard to spoof or dismiss as false contacts" (the speaker never said "dismiss as false contacts"). Resolve context (durability) without inventing content.

================================================================
ISO DATES MANDATORY EVERYWHERE
================================================================

Claim text uses ISO: "2004-11-14" not "14 November 2004". original_excerpt preserves source phrasing.

================================================================
LOCATION_IN_RECORD - most precise span the source allows
================================================================

This document is timestamped (each source line begins with HH:MM:SS.D). Set location_in_record to the timestamp RANGE the claim is drawn from: "HH:MM:SS.D-HH:MM:SS.D" (start of the relevant span to its end), using a plain ASCII hyphen "-" (never an en-dash). A range, not a single point - an assertion spans seconds, and a Q&A claim spans the question and the answer. For a single short line a single timestamp is acceptable.

================================================================
ACRONYM EXPANSION IN CLAIM TEXT
================================================================

Expand each acronym on first use in a claim: "Anomalous Aerial Vehicle (AAV)", "forward-looking infrared (FLIR)". Subsequent uses in the same claim are bare. SAFE acronyms (UFO, UAP, CIA, DoD, etc) are always bare.

================================================================
EXHAUSTIVE EXTRACTION - do not summarise
================================================================

Capture every factual statement, however incidental - dates, names, places, quoted figures, asides, parenthetical remarks. Coverage matters more than highlighting "important" points.

SINCERE ASSERTIONS ONLY - not every sentence is a claim. "Exhaustive" means never SKIM and never curate for importance; it does NOT mean literalise everything spoken. Extract a claim only where the source SINCERELY ASSERTS a fact. The following are NOT claims even though the words appear verbatim in the source, and emitting them as fact is a serious error:

  - JOKES, DEADPAN AND IRONY. A comedic aside, a self-deprecating bit, or a running gag is not biography. Podcast and interview speakers joke about themselves constantly, and deadpan delivery gives no signal that a joke is underway. If a statement would be startling as fact, is played for a laugh, sits oddly against everything else known about the person, or recurs as a running bit, treat it as a JOKE and do not extract it. Example: a host who repeatedly describes himself as "a former pro wrestler who ran for governor" is doing a bit - do NOT emit "X is a former professional wrestler who ran for governor" as an administrative claim about him.
  - HYPOTHETICALS AND RHETORICAL QUESTIONS ("suppose it really was a craft...", "what if they have been here all along?"). The speaker is not asserting it.
  - STATEMENTS QUOTED IN ORDER TO REJECT THEM. When a speaker cites a position to argue AGAINST it, that position is not their claim.
  - HYPERBOLE AND FIGURES OF SPEECH ("a million times faster", "everyone in Washington knows"). Never convert these into measurements or facts.

When unsure whether a statement is sincere, check whether the speaker builds on it as fact anywhere else. If it is never treated as real again, it was not a claim. Omitting a joke costs almost nothing; asserting one as fact manufactures a false fact, which is the worst failure this system can produce.

OPINIONS AND INTERVIEW EXCHANGES: an opinion, assessment, or judgement - especially from an interviewee or expert - is a claim (claim_type "opinion"). When a person gives a short answer, confirmation, or reaction to a question or a stated proposition ("Pretty hard to spoof that", "Every day", "I don't see why not"), it IS a claim: resolve it into a standalone assertion and attribute it to the person who ANSWERED. Do NOT drop a conversational turn because it is brief or depends on the previous line.

  - The speaker is the ANSWERER, not the interviewer. A leading question that contains the fact ("Could it be Russian or Chinese technology?") does NOT make the interviewer the source - the claim and its speaker are the answerer's.
  - For a claim built from a question-and-answer exchange, original_excerpt MUST contain BOTH turns verbatim, each turn prefixed with the speaker's name, so the quote alone proves the claim. Example: original_excerpt = "Bill Whitaker: Could it be Russian or Chinese technology? Ryan Graves: I don't see why not." and content = "Ryan Graves considers Russian or Chinese technology a possible explanation for the observed UAP." (note: content states the assertion with the actor named as SUBJECT - never with a reporting verb like "stated that").
  - location_in_record then spans both turns (start of the question to end of the answer).

If a claim references an entity that does NOT appear in the node directory above, do NOT make up a name for it - either find it in the directory under a different surface form, OR skip that claim. Adding new node names breaks the locked-directory guarantee.

================================================================
ATTESTATION - optional, omit unless there is a clear evidential stance
================================================================

attestation records the evidential standing of a claim. It is OPTIONAL and depends on the
NATURE of the statement, not the speaker's role. Only set it when the claim is an evidential
account of something observed or done:
  - "first_hand": the speaker is stating what they personally did, witnessed, or observed (a pilot describing his own encounter; "I saw the object descend").
  - "second_hand": the speaker is relaying a specific other person's account or observation, or reporting a specific organisation's finding ("David Fravor told me the object accelerated"; "the Pentagon confirmed it cannot identify the objects").
  - "third_hand": the speaker is relaying something passed through an intermediary ("he said the rancher had heard that ...").

OMIT attestation entirely when there is no evidential stance to record - a narrator, host, or
interviewer simply conveying information, framing, or context, or a bare factual statement with
no observer attached. Do NOT default to first_hand. A missing attestation is correct and
expected for most narration. When a researcher or interviewee does inject a genuine first- or
second-hand account into otherwise neutral narration, tag that claim.

================================================================
PROVENANCE_CHAIN - REQUIRED on every claim
================================================================

Every claim MUST carry `provenance_chain`. You cannot emit a claim without answering: who
ORIGINALLY asserted this, and how did it reach the speaker?

  "provenance_chain": {{
    "origin_kind": "speaker|named|anonymous|document|unattributed",
    "origin": "short identification of who first asserted it",
    "origin_ref": "stable handle for a recurring ANONYMOUS actor in THIS record; else \\"\\"",
    "relay": ["ordered steps from origin to speaker; [] when the speaker IS the origin"]
  }}

origin_kind:
  - "speaker"      - the speaker observed it, did it, or holds it themselves. relay: []
  - "named"        - an identifiable person/organisation. It IS a node: also list it in node_references.
  - "anonymous"    - an unnamed or unidentifiable source. It CANNOT be a node, so this field
                     is the ONLY place it survives. Describe the CLAIMED identity without
                     granting it: "a person claiming to work inside the Defense Intelligence
                     Agency" - never "the Defense Intelligence Agency".
  - "document"     - a document or record the speaker reads from or cites.
  - "unattributed" - asserted with no attribution offered (ordinary narration).

CANONICAL PHRASING FOR AN ANONYMOUS ORIGIN - this string is matched across records to detect
when two claims trace back to the SAME source, so phrase it consistently: "a person claiming to
<role or affiliation>". NEVER name the institution as the origin - a CLAIMED affiliation is not
an affiliation, and writing "the Defense Intelligence Agency" converts an anonymous assertion
into an institutional one.

origin_ref - when the record gives a recurring unnamed actor a handle ("the chairman", "my
source"), put a stable slug for that actor here, the same slug on every claim from them. This
is what lets two DIFFERENT anonymous sources in one record be told apart. It identifies them
only WITHIN this record - never assume this record's "the chairman" is another record's source.
Leave it "" for non-anonymous origins.

Worked example - the speaker reads out an email that an intermediary forwarded to him, which
came from an anonymous source claiming to be inside the DIA, whom the record calls "the
chairman":

  "provenance_chain": {{
    "origin_kind": "anonymous",
    "origin": "a person claiming to work inside the Defense Intelligence Agency",
    "origin_ref": "the-chairman",
    "relay": ["an email", "an intermediary known to the speaker"]
  }}

GRADE ATTESTATION BY THE WHOLE CHAIN, not by the last mouth it passed through. If what the
speaker relays reached them THROUGH someone else - a forwarded email, "a friend told me his
contact said", a source repeating what another source told them - that is "third_hand", NOT
"second_hand", even when the speaker names their own immediate contact. Count the removes: if
the person who originally knew the fact is not the speaker, and there is anyone in between, it
is third_hand.

WHY THIS MATTERS AND IS NOT BOOKKEEPING: corroboration is decided by whether two claims share a
chain ROOT. Ten repetitions of one anonymous email are ONE source, not ten. If you drop the
chain, repetition of a single rumour is counted as independent confirmation of it.

================================================================
WHEN THE ATTRIBUTION IS LOAD-BEARING, IT GOES IN THE CLAIM TEXT
================================================================

The rule above - state the bare fact, keep the speaker in the speaker field - holds ONLY when
the speaker is a CONDUIT for something that stands up regardless of who relayed it ("The Nimitz
incident occurred in 2004").

It REVERSES when the claim rests on the assertion itself. If origin_kind is "anonymous", OR
claim_type is "hearsay" or "opinion", OR the attestation is second- or third-hand, then the
attribution MUST appear INSIDE the claim text:

  RIGHT: "An anonymous source claiming to work inside the Defense Intelligence Agency, in an
  email forwarded to Jon Stewart by an intermediary, said the filmed entity was a cloned
  extraterrestrial biological entity type two from the Tau Ceti star system."

  WRONG: "The being was a cloned extraterrestrial biological entity type two from the Tau Ceti
  star system."

The WRONG form is not a tidier version of the RIGHT one - it says something different, and
something false. "The entity came from Tau Ceti" is not a fact about the world and is not what
the source establishes. The fact is THAT AN ANONYMOUS PERSON ASSERTED IT. Strip the attribution
and a true statement about an assertion becomes a false statement about reality. The text is
what gets published; write it so it is still honest when read entirely on its own.

THE ATTRIBUTION DOES NOT REPLACE THE SUBJECT. Naming who asserted a claim NEVER excuses being
vague about what the claim is ABOUT. The claim must still name its subject by the canonical node
name from the directory, exactly as the portability rule requires - a claim has to stand alone
when mixed with claims from a hundred other documents, and "the classified film" identifies
nothing.

  RIGHT: "An anonymous source claiming to work inside the Defense Intelligence Agency, in an
  email forwarded to Jon Stewart by an intermediary, said the being filmed in the Alien
  Interview tape was a cloned extraterrestrial biological entity type two from the Tau Ceti
  star system."

  WRONG: "An anonymous source claimed that the entity in the classified film was a cloned
  extraterrestrial biological entity type two." (WHICH film? Unresolvable out of context.)

Both must be in the sentence: WHO asserted it, and WHAT it is about, by name.

For an opinion, the holder is the subject: "Jon Stewart considers the green-blip account
consistent with a government PSYOP" - never "the green-blip account is a government PSYOP".

ANONYMOUS AND UNNAMED SOURCES - the chain lives in the claim text or nowhere. An anonymous
source can NEVER become a node (see the rule forbidding person nodes for unnamed or redacted
actors), so node_references cannot carry the attribution. The claim TEXT must carry it. When an
assertion arrives from an unnamed source, write the chain into the text:

  RIGHT: "An anonymous source claiming to work inside the Defense Intelligence Agency, in an
  email forwarded to Jon Stewart by an intermediary, said the being filmed was a cloned
  extraterrestrial biological entity type two from the Tau Ceti star system."
  (claim_type "hearsay", attestation "third_hand")

  WRONG: "The being was a cloned extraterrestrial biological entity type two from the Tau Ceti
  star system." (with the DIA merely listed in node_references)

The WRONG form is indistinguishable from the Defense Intelligence Agency having stated it
officially. It converts an anonymous assertion into an institutional fact. Never do this.

RE-STATEMENT IS NOT REPETITION. A source frequently asserts the same proposition more than once
- first as a bare teaser or headline, later with its full attribution. Those are DIFFERENT
claims and you must extract BOTH. The version carrying the richer provenance is the more
valuable one and must NEVER be dropped because a bare version was captured earlier. If you are
about to skip an assertion because "that was already said", check whether the source has since
revealed WHO said it, HOW it reached them, or WHICH document it came from. If any of those is
new, extract it as a separate claim with the fuller attribution.


================================================================
EACH REFERENCE CARRIES A ROLE - what the claim is, not what it names
================================================================

A reference records that a node appears in the claim. It does NOT say the claim is about that node, and those are different things: a claim can name a city because that is where something happened, or name it because the claim is about the city. Give every reference exactly one role, decided by DELETING the node and asking what survives:

  - "subject": delete the node and the claim has no subject left. The claim asserts something about this node.
  - "participant": the node acts in the claim, but the claim is about an event or about another node.
  - "setting": where or when it happened. Delete it and the claim still asserts the same thing, less precisely.
  - "mentioned": context, comparison or aside. Delete it and nothing the claim asserts changes.

Apply the deletion test literally rather than judging importance. "Kevin Day tracked objects from the USS Princeton off San Diego in 2004" gives: Kevin Day subject, USS Princeton participant, San Diego setting. A claim naming a phenomenon the whole record is about - "UFO", "UAP" - is usually "mentioned", because deleting it changes nothing the claim asserts; do not mark it subject merely because it is the record's topic.

Most claims have one subject. A claim with no subject at all is usually a claim that has not been made specific enough.
