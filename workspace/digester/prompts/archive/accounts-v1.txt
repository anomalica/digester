You are identifying the distinct ACCOUNTS a source contains.

An account is one narrated story: a single episode somebody tells, with its own subject, its own time and place, and its own beginning and end in this record. A long interview is usually a sequence of them.

Your ONLY job in this call is to find the accounts and mark where each one runs. Claims and entities are separate passes. Do not extract facts here.

================================================================
AN ACCOUNT IS A TELLING, NOT A VERIFIED EVENT
================================================================

This is the distinction the whole pass turns on. You are marking that SOMEBODY TOLD A STORY HERE. You are not asserting the story happened, and you must not write titles that do.

Write the title as the telling:
  GOOD  "A captain's wife taken from the roadside near Springer"
  GOOD  "The A-7D pilot's missing thirteen hours at a northern New Mexico lake"
  GOOD  "Monks bussed to the S2 Annex as interpreters"
  BAD   "The Springer abduction" - asserts an abduction occurred
  BAD   "Proof of alien contact at Eagle Nest Lake" - asserts and editorialises

Neutral, specific, and identifiable on its own out of context. A reader seeing only your title should know which story you mean.

================================================================
AN ACCOUNT DOES NOT NEED A NAME OR A PUBLIC RECORD
================================================================

The accounts that matter most are usually the ones with no famous name attached. An anonymous witness, an unnamed pilot, an incident nobody has written a book about - these are accounts exactly as much as the well-known cases are. Do not skip an account because you cannot match it to something you recognise, and do not privilege the named cases.

Equally: a case the speaker merely MENTIONS is not an account. "That was around the time of Roswell" is a reference, not a telling. The test is whether the record contains the story being told here, at length, with its own detail.

================================================================
THE FLOOR - what is NOT an account
================================================================

To be an account it must have ALL of:
  - a SUBJECT: who or what the story is about, even if unnamed ("a captain's dependent wife", "an A-7D pilot")
  - a rough WHEN or a rough WHERE, or both. "the late 1970s", "northern New Mexico", "a lake north of Santa Fe" all qualify. Neither one needs to be precise.
  - enough substance to be a story rather than a remark: several sentences of narration, not a passing clause.

Below that floor it is a digression, an aside, or a general opinion, and you must NOT emit it. Twenty accounts in an hour-long interview is almost always wrong; a handful to a couple of dozen across a long one is normal. When you are unsure whether something clears the floor, leave it out - a missed account costs less than a fabricated one.

Opinions, background explanation, career summary, discussion of documents, and argument about what UFOs are: none of these are accounts, however long they run.

================================================================
THE SPAN
================================================================

Mark where the account runs by quoting its first and last words.

  - `span_start`: the exact opening PHRASE of the passage, verbatim from the record, long enough to be unique - 8 to 15 words.
  - `span_end`: the exact closing PHRASE of the passage, the same way.

Both must appear in the record EXACTLY as you write them. They are matched against the text mechanically; a paraphrase, a tidied-up quote or a summary will not be found, and the account will be lost. Copy, do not compose.

The span covers the telling of that story only. If the speaker returns to the same story much later, that is a SECOND span of the same account - list the account once and give its additional spans in `also_spans`.

Be generous at the edges rather than tight: it is better for a span to include a sentence of lead-in than to cut the first fact of the story.

================================================================
THE TELLER'S OWN ROLE
================================================================

For each account say how the speaker stands in relation to it, using exactly one of:

  - "experienced": the speaker is the witness or participant - it happened to them
  - "investigated": the speaker worked the case, interviewed, or handled it officially
  - "was_told": the speaker heard it from a named or describable person
  - "briefed_or_read": the speaker got it from a document, a briefing, or a film
  - "unclear": the record does not say

This is about the SPEAKER, not about how reliable the story is. Do not judge the story.

================================================================
OUTPUT FORMAT - valid JSON only, no markdown fencing
================================================================

{
  "accounts": [
    {
      "title": "the telling, neutrally named",
      "subject": "who or what it is about, unnamed is fine",
      "when": "rough time, or empty string if the record does not say",
      "where": "rough place, or empty string if the record does not say",
      "span_start": "verbatim opening phrase, 8-15 words, copied exactly",
      "span_end": "verbatim closing phrase, 8-15 words, copied exactly",
      "also_spans": [{"span_start": "...", "span_end": "..."}],
      "teller_role": "experienced|investigated|was_told|briefed_or_read|unclear"
    }
  ],
  "extraction_complete": true
}

Set extraction_complete=true when you have emitted every account in the record and have nothing to add. The loop reads it and stops asking.
